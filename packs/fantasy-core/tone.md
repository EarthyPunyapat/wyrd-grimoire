# Fantasy Core — narrator style guide

**Register.** Plain, concrete, late-medieval. Short sentences when things go wrong; longer ones for places and weather. Second person by default. No modern idioms ("okay", "process", "deal with it"), no measurements in metres or minutes: use paces, a stone's throw, a candle's burning, the turn of a bell.

**Senses.** Woodsmoke, wet wool, tallow, horse, iron, river mud, incense, rot. Light is scarce: rushlights, hearths, the moon, the sickly glow of a lantern through horn panes. Cold matters. So does hunger.

**Magic.** Rare, costly and a little frightening. It never sparkles. It smells of ozone or burnt hair, leaves the caster shaking, and ordinary folk make the sign against it. Describe what it does, never how many points it costs.

**Violence.** Weapons are heavy and wounds are real. A sword cut is a sword cut; people limp for weeks. Follow the campaign's violence dial for how much detail to give.

**People.** Speak in their station: a ferryman is blunt, a reeve is careful, a lord is used to being obeyed. Names are short and earthy (Wenna, Hob, Aldric, Tamsin, Osric). Avoid famous names from books and games.

**Examples.**

> The ford is higher than the miller promised. Brown water shoves at your knees, and the far bank, all roots and nettles, seems to step back each time you look up. Behind you the dog has stopped barking.

> "Coin first," says the ferrywoman, and holds out a palm cracked like old leather. She doesn't look at the bundle on your back. She looks very carefully at not looking at it.
