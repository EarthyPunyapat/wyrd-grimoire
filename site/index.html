An RPG engine where the dice actually decide

Wyrd Grimoire is a solo role playing engine that runs on your own machine. Code holds the rules, the sheet and the world. Language models only parse what you type and narrate what the dice returned.

What it does differently

Stakes that cannot be talked away

Most AI game masters drift. They forget the inventory, let you argue past a locked door, and contradict themselves three sessions later. Here the sheet is code, and the dice are real.

Dice you cannot argue with

d100 roll under by default, or d20 with modifiers, returning the same four degrees either way. Strikes land on body locations, armour covers what it covers, and an untreated wound can turn infected.

A world that remembers

Every change is an event in an append only log, and the state is a fold over that log. NPCs know only what they saw, were told, or heard as rumour, so a lie told in one town can reach the captain above the man you lied about.

Your hardware, not a subscription

Text through your ChatGPT plan or DeepSeek. Portraits through local image generation in ComfyUI. Voice through local text to speech. Each campaign is one SQLite file on your disk, and nothing leaves the machine unless you route it to a model.

Any genre, one rules core

Genres are data packs plus small plugins. The fantasy pack ships with 4 ancestries, 6 archetypes, 12 talents, 7 weapons, 4 armour sets, 15 pieces of gear and 11 creatures. A cyberpunk pack is the next proof that the core holds.

Questions

What people ask before running it

Do I need an API key?

Not for the ChatGPT route. You sign in with your own ChatGPT account, and the app keeps the tokens in a secrets folder. DeepSeek and other compatible servers take a key. Ollama takes nothing but a model on disk.

Can I play from my phone or laptop?

Yes. The server runs on the machine with your GPU and serves the browser client over your LAN or Tailscale. A passcode gates it, and settings and usage are per install.

What happens when I lose a fight?

By default, defeat is not death. You are captured, robbed, left for dead or rescued, and the consequence opens a new thread. Hardcore mode is chosen at creation and makes death permanent, with an obituary and a chance to carry the world into a new character.

Which dice system does it use?

d100 roll under is the default, d20 with modifiers is the alternative, and the choice is made per campaign. Both return crit success, success, failure and crit failure, so the rules only ever consume the degree.

Does it work without internet?

It can run entirely local, and there is an offline mode that previews the whole interface without calling a model at all. Tests never reach the network either: they use a fake provider and recorded cassettes.

Where do my campaigns live?

One SQLite file per campaign, with a media folder beside it, under your own home directory. Credentials, tokens and the passcode sit in a separate secrets folder that never reaches a log, an error message or the browser.

How finished is it?

Six of eight milestones are built and runnable: rules, model loop, web client, combat, world and memory, characters and companions. Breadth is next, which brings d20 campaigns, the Living World mode, subsystem toggles and the spellcasting plugin.

What licence is it under?

MIT. Read it, fork it, run it, change the rules in your own pack.
